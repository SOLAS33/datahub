# Writing style for Solas33 sites

Applies to headlines, ledes, meta descriptions, buttons, emails and docs on every Solas33 site (GridWatch, DCWatch, TenderWatch, FirmWatch and any that follow). The aim is plain, specific prose that a researcher or journalist can quote without wincing.

## Rules

1. **Say what the thing is, then what it does.** Lead with the noun. "Company checks from the Irish register" beats "Know who you are dealing with".
2. **Numbers and names instead of adjectives.** "825,173 companies" not "comprehensive coverage". If a figure is not available, say less.
3. **No rhetorical questions** in headlines, and no second-person pitches ("Stay ahead", "Never miss", "Discover", "Unlock").
4. **No slogan structures**: stacked fragments ("Free to check. Pro when it matters."), triplets of matching phrases, "X, not Y" contrasts, and "more than just".
5. **No em dashes.** Use a full stop or a comma. Avoid long semicolon chains: split into sentences.
6. **Banned words:** robust, seamless, leverage, powerful, comprehensive, holistic, insights, landscape, journey, ecosystem, game-changing, cutting-edge, deep dive, at a glance, trusted, transparent (show it instead), empower, streamline, "built for".
7. **Short sentences, active voice, Irish/British spelling.** One idea per sentence. 15 to 20 words is a good average.
8. **Say limits flatly.** "The register has no turnover figures." Not "It's important to note that...".
9. **Name the source** next to any figure that came from one.
10. **Buttons are verbs for the actual action:** "Check a company", "Watch up to 3", "Send enquiry". Not "Get started" or "Learn more".
11. **Pilot/paid wording** states what is sold, the price, the status ("pilot") and what it does not do, in that order.

## Before and after

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Is the company you are about to trust in good standing? | Check an Irish company on the public register |
| Know when a supplier or customer gets into difficulty | Using FirmWatch for suppliers and customers |
| Free to check. Pro when you have a book of suppliers to watch. | Checks, lists and open data are free. Watching a whole supplier list is a paid pilot. |
| A public-data service that turns the register into checks you can read | Company checks from the Irish register, with each rule published |

## Check before publishing

Read the headline aloud. If it sounds like an advert, rewrite it as a label.
